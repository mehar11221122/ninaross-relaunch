What We Look At

The Internal Systems That Decide Whether Hair Grows

Each of these can hold growth back on its own. More often, two of them are working together. Functional medicine reads all of them.

Hormones Estrogen, progesterone, and androgen shifts change how long each hair stays in its growing phase. When the balance moves, density follows.
Thyroid Thyroid function sets the pace of the growth cycle. When it runs slow or fast, hair thins diffusely across the whole head.
Nutrients Iron stores, vitamin D, B12, and protein feed the follicle. Low stores can hold growth back even when everything else looks fine.
Metabolism Blood sugar and insulin influence androgen activity, one of the common drivers behind crown and part-line thinning.
Gut Health Absorption decides whether the nutrients you take in actually reach the follicle. This is often why supplements alone never move the needle.
Toxicity Load Ongoing exposures add to the body's total burden. When that burden is high, the body deprioritizes hair growth first.
Inflammation Inflammation around the follicle is one of the few drivers visible under magnification. Calming it usually has to come before regrowth work begins.
Food Reactivity Foods that quietly keep the immune system activated can keep low-grade inflammation running, showing up on the scalp before anywhere else.
Neurotransmitters Stress chemistry shifts the growth cycle in bulk. A hard season often shows up in the brush two to three months later.

What Most Pages Leave Out

When Two Different Causes Look The Same From Outside

A hormonally driven pattern and a nutrient-driven pattern can look identical in the mirror. Both give you more scalp at the part, more hair in the brush, and a thinner ponytail. From arm's length there is nothing to separate them. That is why so much time gets spent on the wrong plan.

Under 200x magnification, they separate. An androgen-driven pattern shows mixed shaft thickness in a small area, thick hairs beside noticeably finer ones, concentrated at the crown and part line. A nutrient or stress-driven shed shows hairs of even caliber thinning across the whole head, with more empty follicle openings, and it usually has a start date you can point to.

That distinction changes what is worth testing. Mixed-caliber at the crown sends us toward hormones and metabolism. Even, whole-head thinning sends us toward iron, thyroid, and what has been happening in the last three to six months. Inflammation visible around the openings changes the order entirely, because calming that comes before any growth work.

It also tells us what is honest. Where follicle openings are still intact, there is something to work with. Where the scalp shows smooth areas with no openings, that tissue has scarred and regrowth is off the table. We tell you that clearly so your time and resources go toward the areas that can respond.

What is functional medicine for hair loss?

Functional medicine looks at the internal systems driving your hair loss: hormones, thyroid, nutrients, gut health, inflammation, and stress. Instead of treating the scalp alone, we identify the root cause and build a plan around it.

How is functional medicine different from dermatology for hair loss?

Dermatology focuses on the skin at the scalp. Functional medicine goes deeper: the hormonal, metabolic, nutritional, and immune factors that determine whether your follicles can grow. We do both in the same visit.

What systems does the functional medicine assessment look at?

Hormones, thyroid function, iron and ferritin, vitamin D, gut health, inflammation, metabolism, toxicity load, stress chemistry, and food reactivity. Each one can hold hair growth back on its own.

Will I need blood work?

Your Discovery report may recommend specific panels based on what your scalp read and history show. We tell you which labs to request from your doctor, or we can order them here.

How long until I see results from a functional medicine approach?

It varies. Some clients see changes within 8 to 12 weeks. Full results can take 6 to 12 months depending on the driver. We photograph from fixed positions every visit so progress is measured, documented, and real.

Can functional medicine help if I have already tried everything?

Often, yes. Products and supplements address the surface. Functional medicine reaches the hormonal, metabolic, and immune layers that products cannot touch. If the driver is deeper, that is where the answer is.

Do I need a referral?
