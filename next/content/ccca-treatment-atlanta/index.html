Before It Looks Like Anything.

By the time the center of your crown shows in a photo, CCCA has usually been working for a while. The early version is quieter. It gets blamed on the style, the weather, or stress, and it gets missed.

Here is what to notice, and what we look for at 200x.

01 A tender spot at the very top Tingling, burning or soreness at the center of the crown that comes and goes, often worse on wash day.

Why CCCA Affects Black Women Disproportionately

CCCA is the most common scarring alopecia in Black women. It starts at the center of the crown and moves outward in a widening circle, which is what "centrifugal" means. The reasons it shows up in us more than anyone else are still being studied, and honest people say so.

You shouldn't have to explain any of this to the person treating it.

What We Know

It runs in families. Researchers have identified inherited factors in some of them. It involves inflammation around the follicle that eventually replaces the follicle with scar tissue. And it tends to be found late, because the crown is the one place you cannot see without a second mirror.

What Has Been Studied

Certain grooming practices have been associated with CCCA in some studies, although CCCA is multifactorial and these practices have not been established as the sole cause.

What It Is Not

It is not your fault. You styled your hair the way you were taught, the way most of us were. We will ask about relaxers, braids, wigs and heat because the history helps us read the scalp. Nothing about it is on trial here.

How We Diagnose CCCA: Trichoscopy Versus Biopsy.

A biopsy answers the question by removing tissue. Trichoscopy answers a lot of it by magnifying your scalp 200 times, live, while you watch, and it can be done at your first visit.

Early CCCA At 200x We look for peripilar white or grey haloes around the hair shafts at the crown, single hairs where you should see groupings of two and three, and a loss of the normal follicular openings in the middle of the vertex. The pattern radiates outward from the center.
Traction Alopecia At 200x Traction sits at the margins rather than the vertex, spares a fringe of hair at the very front, and shows retained openings with broken hairs and casts along the shafts. Openings that are still there mean follicles that are still there.
Lichen Planopilaris At 200x LPP shows tubular scaling that wraps the emerging shaft and a violet tone around the follicle, often scattered across the scalp rather than radiating from the crown. That scaling is the detail that separates it from CCCA before either one has scarred visibly.

Reading those three apart early is the whole game, because the treatment plan for each one is different and the window for protecting active follicles is not open forever. When trichoscopy leaves a real question, a biopsy is the right next step and we will tell you so plainly. Everything else is handled in-house.

Can hair grow back after CCCA?

Partly, and the honest answer depends on the follicle. Follicles that have already scarred do not regrow, and no treatment changes that. Follicles at the edge of the affected area that are still active can often be protected and can fill back in. That is exactly what the 200x read is for: telling you which is which before you spend a year hoping. Results not typical. Individual results will vary.

How do you know it's CCCA and not something else?

At 200x we look for peripilar haloes around the shafts at the crown, single hairs where groupings should be, and loss of follicular openings radiating outward from the vertex. Traction alopecia sits at the margins with a retained fringe. Lichen planopilaris shows tubular scaling wrapping the emerging shaft. When trichoscopy leaves a real question, a biopsy is the right next step and we will say so.

Do I need a biopsy before I come see you?

No. Come as you are, with or without a diagnosis. We start with your history and a 200x read of your scalp, and if a biopsy would change the plan we will tell you that at the visit.

I was told it's CCCA and given a prescription. What else is there?

A plan. Naming the condition is the first part. The second part is calming the inflammation around the follicle, protecting the areas that are still active, and reading the whole-body factors that show up often in this population, such as thyroid, iron and blood sugar.
